# Languages: what RuneManga reads and what it translates into

**[← Back to the README](../README.md)** · **[Features & plans](features.md)** · **[Website](https://runemanga.com/?utm_source=github&utm_medium=readme&utm_campaign=runemanga-public)**

RuneManga reads comic lettering in **11 source languages** and translates it into **89 target languages**. Every language is open on every plan, Free included. This page lists them all, explains which ones are experimental, and covers the languages the website and the extension themselves are available in.

Three things are true of every language below:

- **The original language is detected automatically.** There is no source menu to set: the language on the page is recognised among the 11 sources.
- **Only speech-bubble dialogue is translated today.** Narration boxes and loose text on the art are not translated yet, and sound effects stay as drawn on purpose.
- **The same translator on every plan.** RuneLingua, RuneManga's own AI translator, writes the translation, with Google Translate for a few targets it does not write well yet.

## Japanese manga

Japanese manga is read from right to left and its lettering is usually vertical. RuneManga reads each bubble on its own and puts the translation back into that same bubble, set horizontally, so the order of the bubbles stays the one you know. You only need to send the pages in order, page one first: nothing has to be reversed.

- **Vertical text** is one of the hardest cases for text recognition and works best on large, clean scans.
- **Furigana**, the small kana printed beside kanji, can blur into the main text on a low-resolution page. A larger scan is the fix.
- **Honorifics** such as -san, -kun, -chan and -senpai may be kept, translated or dropped, and machine translation is not always consistent about them from page to page.
- **Names** are transliterated, so the same name can appear in slightly different spellings across pages.

## Korean manhwa and webtoons

Manhwa is read left to right and today is mostly published as webtoons: long, full-colour vertical strips. Translate changes only the lettering, so the colours stay exactly as they are.

- **Hangul** is built from small blocks of strokes, so resolution matters more than for most scripts.
- **Lettering on the art** with no bubble around it is common in webtoons, and text outside bubbles is not translated yet.
- **Forms of address** such as -ssi, -nim, sunbae, hyung and oppa have no direct English equivalent and are translated, kept or dropped.
- **Very tall strips** count by their full size: split a strip at a gap between panels if an upload is refused.

📖 **[How to translate Korean manhwa to English](https://runemanga.com/guides/translate-korean-manhwa-to-english)** · **[Manhwa and webtoons](https://runemanga.com/guides/translate-manhwa-and-webtoons)** · **[Use case: manhwa and webtoons](https://runemanga.com/use-cases/manhwa-webtoons)**

## Chinese manhua

Manhua can be printed in Simplified or Traditional characters, and both are sources. Most current titles read left to right, often as full-colour vertical-scroll webcomics.

- **No spaces between words**, so a single misread character can change a whole phrase. Resolution matters.
- **Names and fixed terms**, such as cultivation ranks and technique names, can be rendered in different ways on different pages. Keep a short list of the names you see.
- **Stylised titles and sound effects** are drawn as part of the artwork and are left as they are.

📖 **[How to translate Chinese manhua to English](https://runemanga.com/guides/translate-chinese-manhua-to-english)** · **[Use case: manhua](https://runemanga.com/use-cases/manhua)**

## English and the other sources

RuneManga is not only for Japanese and Korean into English. An English comic can be read in Spanish, a French one in German, a Thai one in Indonesian: any of the 11 sources goes into any of the 89 targets. A page that is already in the language you picked is not charged.

## Experimental targets: Arabic and Thai

**Arabic and Thai output is experimental: their lettering is not verified yet.** You can pick them, and the pickers tag them *(experimental)*. Both scripts need more room for their shapes and marks, and Arabic is written right to left, so check the result more closely than you would for a Latin-script language.

## Which translator writes each language

| Case | Translator |
|---|---|
| Almost every target, on every plan | **RuneLingua**, RuneManga's own AI translator for manga and manhwa dialogue, on our servers |
| A few targets RuneLingua does not write well yet, such as Swahili, Zulu and Quechua | **Google Translate** |
| A page translated while RuneLingua is briefly unavailable | **Google Translate**, so a chapter does not stop halfway |
| In the extension, if you pick Google Translate as the model (next extension update) | **Google Translate**, in your browser |
| In the extension, if you connect your own AI key (next extension update) | **Your provider**, with the model you chose |

In every case, only the text found on the page is translated, never the image. RuneLingua is new and still improving; we do not claim it beats Google Translate, so judge it on your own series.

## Why some languages need more room in a bubble

Japanese, Chinese and Korean pack a lot of meaning into few characters, so a bubble with six characters can need a full sentence in English. Spanish, Portuguese, French, German, Russian and Turkish tend to need even more space than English. Bubbles are drawn for the original text, and a tall, narrow bubble made for vertical Japanese has little room for horizontal lines.

RuneManga starts from the size of the original lettering and shrinks the text until it fits inside the bubble, wrapping on word boundaries and hyphenating a very long word rather than letting the text get too small. On a very small bubble the result is small text, not text running over the art. If you read several languages, a shorter one usually fits better.

📖 **[Why translated text does not fit the bubble](https://runemanga.com/guides/translated-text-does-not-fit-the-bubble)**
